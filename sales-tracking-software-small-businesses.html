Why sales records fall apart in small businesses

Most small businesses track sales in a receipt book, a notebook, or a memory of "who owes what." It works for a day or two, then a page goes missing, a customer disputes a debt, or the owner simply can't say what actually moved that week without counting stock by hand.

Spreadsheet tools don't fix this either — they assume a desk, a laptop, and a stable connection, none of which match a shop counter or depot floor. Sales tracking that actually holds up needs to work exactly where the sale happens, with no signal required.

What good sales tracking software should do

Whether it's built into a depot app or a full accounting suite, sales tracking that works for a small business needs the same core pieces.

Real-time sales logging

Credit and debtor tracking

Staff accounts and permissions

Printable reports

Daily, weekly, and custom-range sales reports ready to print or share, no spreadsheet required.

Frequently asked questions

Does sales tracking work without internet?

Yes. Both My Depot Master and SME Accounts Pro are offline-first — log sales with no signal, and they sync automatically once you're back online.

Can I track customer credit and debts, not just cash sales?

Yes. Both apps include a credit book so you always know who owes what.

Which app should I choose — My Depot Master or SME Accounts Pro?

Choose My Depot Master if you mainly need inventory and sales tracking for a depot or shop. Choose SME Accounts Pro if you also need a full ledger, tax records, and payroll.

Can I bill customers with mobile money?

Yes. Billing is built around Airtel Money, MTN MoMo, and Zamtel Kwacha.
